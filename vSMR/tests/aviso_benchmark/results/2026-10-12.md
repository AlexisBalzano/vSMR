# Recorded AVISO component benchmark — 2026-10-12

The x86 Release harness completed successfully on the user's Ryzen 7 9800X3D
machine. It used Windows 11 Home build 26200, MSVC 19.51.36256.0 / v145, and real
GDI+ rendering. The machine has an RTX 5070 and approximately 32 GB of memory;
the targets in this benchmark are off-screen DIBs. Builds and the main regression
suite were kept idle during timing. EuroScope was not running.

The original renderer was preserved from `dev` commit `e774ac5`. The optimized
renderer was the `FPS-Killer` working tree identified by source hashes in
[the run metadata](benchmark_2026_10_12.json). The full 140-row statistics are in
[the result CSV](2026-10-12.csv). See [the harness README](../README.md) for the
request construction, rendering boundaries and reproduction command.

Forty alternating rebuild sample pairs followed three warmup pairs. Each
presentation operation used 160 sample pairs. Twenty scenes covered LFPG, LFPO
and LFML in Dark, Light and Real palettes, with whole/detail views plus two LFPG
3840 x 2160 raster cases. Detailed scenes rotate the projection 23 degrees.

## Rebuild results

Representative Dark-palette results:

| Scene | Mean before (ms) | Mean after (ms) | p95 before (ms) | p95 after (ms) | Time reduction |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| LFPG whole | 43.758 | 36.266 | 46.071 | 38.847 | 17.1% |
| LFPG detail, rotated | 11.917 | 10.054 | 12.121 | 10.180 | 15.6% |
| LFPG whole, 3840 x 2160 cache | 67.590 | 59.586 | 68.492 | 60.282 | 11.8% |
| LFPG detail, 3840 x 2160 cache | 22.319 | 20.339 | 22.813 | 20.876 | 8.9% |
| LFPO whole | 10.234 | 8.800 | 10.436 | 8.941 | 14.0% |
| LFPO detail, rotated | 8.692 | 7.458 | 8.951 | 7.603 | 14.2% |
| LFML whole | 3.594 | 2.841 | 3.790 | 2.953 | 20.9% |
| LFML detail, rotated | 15.144 | 12.009 | 15.330 | 12.318 | 20.7% |

Across all 18 standard 1920 x 1080 cases, warm rebuild mean time fell 13.7–21.2%
(1.16–1.27x). First-render and scene-construction costs are recorded separately
in the CSV. Spatial culling has less impact when the entire map is visible.
For example, LFPG detail queried 53 of 442 features and 98 of 1034 labels, skipped
1240 out-of-view paths, and projected 5020 vertices. LFPG whole queried all features
and labels and projected 26812 vertices.

Pan/zoom replay deliberately changes bounds at each render. Its mean changes
ranged from a 3.0% increase to a 3.1% reduction across standard scenes: essentially
unchanged. LFPG Dark whole was 42.657 → 42.547 ms. Rebuilding glyphs at the exact
new origin preserves original antialias samples, so the warm same-view glyph-cache
gain does not apply to these moved views. These measurements do not establish a
large improvement in raster rebuild latency during continuous pan/zoom.

## Cached presentation results

Across standard scenes:

| Operation | Mean component speedup | Mean time reduction |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Clear + AlphaBlend → clear + opaque BitBlt | 5.23–5.94x | 80.9–83.2% |
| Clear + AlphaBlend → covered opaque BitBlt | 10.05–10.72x | 90.0–90.7% |
| Clear + scaled AlphaBlend → covered opaque StretchBlt | 4.27–4.44x | 76.6–77.5% |

For LFPG Dark whole, covered native presentation was 1.401 → 0.132 ms, with p95
1.460 → 0.145 ms. Covered scaled presentation was 1.923 → 0.436 ms, with p95
2.105 → 0.456 ms. The conservative clear-plus-BitBlt comparison was 1.400 → 0.239 ms.

Copying the larger 3840 x 2160 LFPG whole bitmap showed a smaller covered native
gain: 6.843 → 1.774 ms (3.86x), with p95 7.393 → 2.237 ms. Its scaled copy to the
1379 x 777 target was 1.980 → 0.456 ms (4.34x). These larger native copies are
reported separately from standard display-size timings.

## Fidelity and scope

All 20 original/optimized transparent rasters matched exactly. All 240 pan/zoom
replay comparisons and all 20 changed visibility/zoom/scaling/skew-projection
comparisons matched exactly. Native and scaled opaque-cache comparisons differed
by at most 2/255 in an RGB channel, from GDI+ source-over versus Windows AlphaBlend
integer rounding. There were no image-check failures; the executable returned zero.

The principal confirmed gain is cached map presentation. Warm raster rebuilds
improved moderately; moving-view rebuilds still cost roughly their baseline time.
These are component measurements. They do not establish 200/240 FPS, 1% low FPS,
EuroScope frame pacing, a host FPS cap, input-to-display latency, real-window driver
cost or full plug-in CPU utilization. Measuring those requires an active EuroScope
scene and the plug-in diagnostics. Geographic overscan margins and host cache-sizing
choices also need validation in that environment.
